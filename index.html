What is GEO, and how can a company measure it? GEOとは何か。企業はAI検索での見え方をどう測るべきか

GEO is not just another SEO acronym. It is the practice of understanding how AI answer engines describe, compare, and recommend your brand. GEOは単なるSEO用語ではありません。AI回答エンジンがブランドをどう説明し、比較し、推薦しているかを把握するための考え方です。

Search is no longer only a page of links.

People still use Google. They still compare websites, read reviews, and ask colleagues. But a growing part of discovery now happens inside AI answer engines: ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and other systems that do not simply return pages. They read, summarize, compare, and answer.

That changes the question for brands.

It is no longer enough to ask, "Do we rank?" A second question now matters: "When someone asks an AI about this category, are we present in the answer, and are we described correctly?"

That question is the beginning of GEO.

GEO means Generative Engine Optimization

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization. It is the practice of understanding and improving how AI answer engines describe, compare, and recommend brands, products, services, and ideas.

The word can sound more complicated than the work itself. At its core, GEO asks a practical question:

When a buyer asks an AI for help, what does the AI say?

Does it mention your brand? Does it understand what you do? Does it compare you with the right competitors? Does it recommend you for the situations where you are actually a good fit? Does it use current, specific language, or does it repeat vague descriptions from old pages and secondhand sources?

GEO is not about trying to trick AI systems. It is about making your brand easier to understand, easier to verify, and easier to cite.

How GEO is different from SEO

SEO usually starts with pages and rankings. A team chooses keywords, improves pages, earns links, and measures whether a page appears in search results.

GEO starts with answers.

The buyer may never see a list of ten links. They may ask, "What are the best tools for monitoring AI visibility in Japan?" or "Which skincare brands are good for sensitive skin?" or "What should a mid-sized B2B company use for customer data activation?" The AI then produces an answer that may include explanations, comparisons, recommendations, and citations.

In SEO, the user chooses which result to click.

In AI answers, the system has already made a first selection. It decides which entities to mention, which differences to highlight, and which sources seem useful enough to support the answer.

That does not make SEO obsolete. It makes the brand visibility layer wider. A company still needs strong pages, clear content, and technical accessibility. But it also needs to understand whether those assets are being interpreted correctly inside generated answers.

What companies should measure

A first GEO diagnosis does not need to be complicated. It should measure a small set of things clearly and repeatedly.

Brand coverage. Does your brand appear in relevant AI answers at all? Coverage is the simplest visibility signal. If buyers ask category-level questions and your brand never appears, the issue is not wording. The issue is absence.

Share of voice. When your brand does appear, how often does it appear compared with competitors? AI visibility is relative. A brand can appear occasionally and still be less visible than a competitor that appears across more prompts, more contexts, and more answer engines.

Recommendation rate. Mentions are not the same as recommendations. An AI answer may name your brand neutrally, mention it as one option, or actively recommend it for a specific use case. Those differences matter.

Positioning accuracy. Does the AI describe the brand in a way the company would recognize? Sometimes the problem is not invisibility. It is an outdated category, an incomplete audience definition, or a description that misses the reason the brand exists.

Competitor mentions. Which competitors appear most often, and in what contexts? This helps a team see whether AI systems associate the category with a different set of players than the company expects.

Content gaps. Which questions reveal weak or missing source material? If AI answers are vague, wrong, or overly dependent on third-party descriptions, that often points to content that needs to be clarified on the brand's own site.

How to run a simple first diagnosis

The first step is to choose the category. A brand should not start with every possible question. Start with one market, one language, and one buying context.

Next, define the comparison set. Include direct competitors, adjacent alternatives, and sometimes the category leaders that buyers are likely to know. AI answer engines do not organize the market according to your internal sales deck. They organize it around what they can read and what users ask.

Then write buyer prompts. These should sound like real questions, not keyword lists. Good prompts include category discovery, comparison, use case, budget, audience, geography, and decision criteria.

For example:

  • "What tools can help a marketing team measure whether its brand appears in AI answers?"
  • "Which agencies can support AI search visibility for Japanese and English content?"
  • "How should a B2B SaaS company check whether ChatGPT recommends its competitors?"

After that, test across multiple AI answer engines. A single model is not enough. Different systems use different sources, have different answer styles, and may produce different recommendations.

Finally, collect the answers and compare them. Do not only read them once. Preserve the prompt, model, answer, cited sources, brand mentions, competitor mentions, recommendation language, and useful evidence. The point is not to win one prompt. The point is to see patterns.

Why manual checking is not enough

Manual checking is useful for intuition. It is not enough for reporting.

There are too many prompts. A brand may need to test dozens of buyer questions across categories, languages, and journey stages.

Answers vary by model. A brand may be visible in one answer engine and absent in another. A competitor may be recommended in one context and ignored in the next.

Evidence needs to be preserved. Marketing teams need to know not only the score, but what the AI actually said and why that answer matters.

Teams also need repeatability. A CMO, SEO lead, content strategist, or agency client cannot make decisions from screenshots gathered ad hoc. They need a structured way to compare visibility over time.

GEO turns visibility into a signal

The useful version of GEO is not another acronym to add to a strategy deck. It is a way to turn a vague concern into a measurable marketing signal.

Are we present?

Are we understood?

Are we recommended?

Are our competitors being explained more clearly than we are?

Once those questions can be measured, teams can act. They can improve content, adjust positioning, clarify product pages, build better comparison material, and create source material that reflects what buyers actually ask.

That is the practical value of GEO. It helps a company see how it exists inside AI-mediated discovery, then decide what to improve.

GEOはGenerative Engine Optimizationのこと

GEOはGenerative Engine Optimizationの略です。AI回答エンジンが、ブランド、商品、サービス、考え方をどのように説明し、比較し、推薦しているかを把握し、改善していくための実践です。

言葉だけを見ると少し難しく感じるかもしれません。ただ、中心にある問いはとても実務的です。

購買者がAIに相談したとき、AIは何と答えているのか。

自社ブランドは言及されているのか。何をしている会社として理解されているのか。比較対象は適切か。本当に強みを持つ用途で推薦されているのか。現在の正確な情報にもとづいて説明されているのか、それとも古いページや第三者の曖昧な説明をもとに要約されているのか。

GEOはAIをだますためのものではありません。ブランドを、AIにも人にも理解しやすく、検証しやすく、引用しやすい状態に整えるための考え方です。

GEOとSEOの違い

SEOは、多くの場合、ページと順位から始まります。キーワードを選び、ページを改善し、リンクや技術要件を整え、検索結果でどの位置に表示されるかを見ます。

GEOは、回答から始まります。

購買者は、リンク一覧を見ないかもしれません。「日本でAI可視性を測定するにはどんなツールがあるか」「敏感肌に向いているスキンケアブランドはどれか」「中堅B2B企業が顧客データ活用を始めるなら何を使うべきか」とAIに聞くかもしれません。AIは、説明、比較、推薦、引用を含む回答を返します。

SEOでは、ユーザーがどの検索結果をクリックするかを選びます。

AI回答では、その前段階でシステムが一度選んでいます。どのブランドを言及するか、どの違いを強調するか、どの情報源を信頼できるものとして扱うかを、回答の中で判断しています。

これはSEOが不要になるという意味ではありません。むしろ、ブランド可視性の層が広がったということです。企業には今まで通り、強いページ、明確なコンテンツ、読み取りやすいサイト構造が必要です。そのうえで、それらが生成回答の中で正しく解釈されているかを見る必要があります。

企業が測るべきもの

最初のGEO診断は、複雑である必要はありません。重要なのは、少数の指標を明確に、繰り返し測ることです。

ブランドカバレッジ。 関連するAI回答の中に、自社ブランドがそもそも出てくるか。これは最も基本的な可視性指標です。カテゴリーについて聞かれているのに一度も出てこない場合、問題は表現ではなく不在です。

シェア・オブ・ボイス。 自社ブランドが出てくる頻度は、競合と比べてどうか。AI可視性は相対的です。たまに言及されていても、競合がより多くのPrompt、文脈、回答エンジンで登場しているなら、存在感には差があります。

推薦率。 言及と推薦は同じではありません。AIはブランドを中立的に名前だけ出すこともあれば、選択肢の一つとして紹介することも、特定用途に明確に推薦することもあります。この違いは重要です。

ポジショニングの正確性。 AIの説明は、自社が認識しているブランド像と一致しているか。問題は「出てこないこと」だけではありません。古いカテゴリーで説明されている、対象顧客がずれている、存在理由が伝わっていない、ということもあります。

競合言及。 どの競合が、どの文脈で多く出てくるか。AI回答が市場をどのようなプレイヤーで構成しているかを見ることで、自社の想定とは違う競争環境が見えることがあります。

コンテンツギャップ。 どの問いで、情報不足や誤解が起きているか。AI回答が曖昧だったり、第三者情報に強く依存していたりする場合、自社サイト側で明確にすべき情報が不足している可能性があります。

最初の診断の進め方

まずはカテゴリーを絞ります。最初からすべての問いを扱う必要はありません。一つの市場、一つの言語、一つの購買文脈から始めます。

次に、比較対象を定義します。直接競合だけでなく、隣接する代替手段や、購買者が思い浮かべやすいカテゴリーリーダーも含めます。AI回答エンジンは、社内の営業資料通りに市場を整理しているわけではありません。読める情報と、ユーザーが聞く問いをもとに整理します。

その後、購買者らしいPromptを作ります。キーワードの羅列ではなく、実際に人が相談しそうな問いにします。カテゴリー発見、比較、用途、予算、対象者、地域、判断基準などを含めると有効です。

たとえば、次のような問いです。

  • 「マーケティングチームが、自社ブランドがAI回答に出ているかを測るにはどんなツールがあるか」
  • 「日本語と英語のAI検索可視性を支援できる会社はどこか」
  • 「B2B SaaS企業は、ChatGPTが競合を推薦しているかどう確認すべきか」

次に、複数のAI回答エンジンで確認します。一つのモデルだけでは十分ではありません。システムごとに参照する情報、回答の癖、推薦の出方が異なります。

最後に、回答を保存し、比較します。一度読んで終わりにしないことが大切です。Prompt、モデル、回答本文、引用元、自社ブランドの言及、競合の言及、推薦表現、根拠として使える箇所を残します。目的は一つのPromptで勝つことではありません。パターンを見ることです。

手作業の確認だけでは足りない理由

手作業の確認は、感覚をつかむには役立ちます。ただ、レポートとしては不十分です。

Promptの数が多すぎます。ブランドによっては、カテゴリー、言語、購買段階ごとに数十の問いを確認する必要があります。

回答はモデルによって変わります。あるAI回答エンジンでは見えていても、別のエンジンでは出てこないことがあります。ある文脈では競合が推薦され、別の文脈では無視されることもあります。

証拠を残す必要もあります。マーケティングチームが必要としているのは、スコアだけではありません。AIが実際に何と答えたのか、その回答がなぜ重要なのかです。

そして、再現性も必要です。CMO、SEO担当、コンテンツ担当、代理店のクライアントは、その場で集めたスクリーンショットだけでは判断できません。時間を置いて比較できる、構造化されたレポートが必要です。

GEOは可視性を測定可能なシグナルに変える

GEOの実務的な価値は、新しい略語を増やすことではありません。漠然とした不安を、測定可能なマーケティングシグナルに変えることです。

これらの問いを測れるようになると、チームは具体的に動けます。コンテンツを改善し、ポジショニングを見直し、商品ページを明確にし、比較情報を整え、購買者が実際に聞く問いに答える情報源を作ることができます。

それがGEOの実務的な意味です。AIを介した発見の中で、自社がどう存在しているかを見えるようにし、次に何を改善すべきかを決めるためのものです。

Beacon GEO: turning AI answers into a brand visibility report Beacon GEO：AI回答をブランド可視性レポートに変える

Beacon GEO helps turn buyer prompts, AI answers, and competitor mentions into a structured report for marketing and content teams. Beacon GEOは、購買者のPrompt、AI回答、競合言及を、マーケティングチームが使える構造化レポートへ変換します。

Most marketing teams now have a few stories about AI answers.

Someone asked ChatGPT about the category and noticed a competitor appeared first. Someone tried Perplexity and found an outdated description of the brand. Someone asked a buyer-style question and saw that the AI recommended tools the team had not considered direct competitors.

Those moments are useful. They create urgency.

But they are not a reporting system.

A screenshot can start a conversation, but it cannot tell a team how often the issue happens, which prompts matter, which competitors are gaining visibility, or what content should be improved first.

Beacon GEO was built for that gap: turning buyer prompts, AI answers, and competitor mentions into a structured AI Visibility Report.

What the report answers

An AI Visibility Report should make the invisible parts of AI discovery easier to discuss.

It starts with simple questions.

Does AI mention our brand?

This is the baseline. If relevant buyer prompts do not surface the brand, the team needs to know where the absence happens: by category, use case, geography, language, or model.

Does AI recommend us?

A brand mention is not always meaningful. A recommendation signal is stronger. The report looks for moments when the AI presents the brand as a good option, a preferred choice, or a relevant solution for a specific buyer need.

Which competitors appear more often?

AI answers often reveal the competitive set that exists in the model's view of the market. Sometimes it matches the team's expectations. Sometimes it does not. Either way, it is useful evidence.

What language does AI use to describe each player?

The wording matters. AI may describe one company with concrete use cases and another with vague category language. It may connect a competitor to stronger proof points, clearer audiences, or more current terminology.

Which prompts reveal content gaps?

Some prompts produce weak answers because the available source material is thin. Others expose missing comparison pages, unclear service descriptions, outdated product language, or multilingual inconsistency. The report should point to those gaps, not just list scores.

How Beacon GEO works

Beacon GEO is used as the working tool behind the report. The public offer is simple: UTAKATA LAB runs a structured AI Visibility Report for a brand, category, and competitor set.

The workflow begins by defining the project. We set the brand, competitors, market, language, and category boundaries. This matters because AI visibility is always contextual. A brand may be visible in one market and absent in another. It may appear for educational prompts but not for buying prompts.

Next, we generate or import a prompt set. The prompts are written to resemble real buyer questions, not internal keyword lists. They can cover category discovery, vendor comparison, use cases, industry context, local market needs, objections, and decision criteria.

Then the prompt set is reviewed and calibrated. This step is important. A prompt that is too broad produces generic answers. A prompt that is too narrow may only confirm what the team already knows. The useful middle is specific enough to reflect buyer intent and broad enough to reveal market patterns.

After that, Beacon GEO collects answers from selected AI models. The goal is not to ask one system once. It is to create a comparable dataset across prompts, entities, and answer engines.

The answers are then analyzed for coverage, share of voice, recommendation signals, and entity comparison. This turns a pile of AI responses into a report that a marketing team can actually use.

Finally, the data and findings are exported into a practical summary: what is visible, what is missing, what competitors are associated with, and what content opportunities should be prioritized.

What the dashboard shows

The Beacon GEO dashboard is designed to make the report explainable.

At the aggregate level, it shows brand visibility across the selected prompt set. This helps teams understand the overall shape of the issue before reading individual answers.

The track dashboard shows how prompts, models, and entities relate to each other. A team can see where the brand appears, where competitors appear, and where answers are thin or unexpected.

Prompt evidence preserves the raw answer behind the finding. This is essential. A score without the original answer is hard to trust. The evidence allows a team to read what the AI actually said and judge whether the interpretation is useful.

Entity comparison shows how brands and competitors are described across the dataset. It helps reveal differences in positioning, proof, audience, and recommendation language.

Together, these views help the report stay grounded. The point is not to create a mysterious score. The point is to connect every finding back to real prompts and real generated answers.

Who the report is for

The report is useful for teams that need to make decisions about brand visibility, content, and positioning.

For a CMO or VP Marketing, it provides a concise view of whether the brand is present in AI-assisted discovery and whether competitors are being recommended more clearly.

For an SEO or GEO team, it adds a new layer of visibility measurement alongside search performance, technical health, and content coverage.

For a content marketing team, it identifies where existing content is not answering buyer questions clearly enough, and where new pages or clearer explanations may be needed.

For an agency or consultant, it creates a structured way to show clients what AI answer engines currently say and which actions are worth taking next.

For sales enablement, it can reveal how AI systems explain the category, the competitors, and the buying criteria that prospects may already have seen before a sales conversation begins.

The useful output is not just the score

The most useful part of an AI Visibility Report is not a single number.

It is the combination of score, evidence, and next step.

A team needs to know that the brand appeared in 30 percent of relevant prompts. But it also needs to know which prompts those were, what the answer said, how competitors were framed, and what should be changed in the brand's own content.

That is where Beacon GEO is most useful. It does not treat AI visibility as a vague impression. It turns it into something a team can read, compare, and discuss.

For some brands, the first report may show that the problem is absence. The brand is not appearing in category answers at all.

For others, the issue may be accuracy. The brand appears, but the description is old, generic, or attached to the wrong use case.

For others, the issue may be competitive framing. Competitors are recommended with clearer proof, while the brand is mentioned without a reason to choose it.

Each of those problems requires a different response. A structured report helps separate them.

AIは各ブランドをどのような言葉で説明しているか。

表現は重要です。ある企業は具体的な用途とともに説明され、別の企業は曖昧なカテゴリー語だけで説明されることがあります。競合の方が、より明確な実績、対象顧客、現在の用語と結びついている場合もあります。

どのPromptがコンテンツギャップを示しているか。

What does an AI say about your brand? あなたのブランドについて、AIは何と言うか

Try this. Open an AI and ask it a question about your brand or product. Ask it to describe what the brand does, who it's for, what makes it different. Read the answer.

Is your brand and product in the answer? Is it accurate? Is it the version the brand would recognize? Or is it a plausible-sounding summary assembled from whatever the AI had access to — a mix of old press releases, review sites, and someone's blog post from 2021?

Most brands don't know it.

The change that's already happened

Search behavior is shifting. Not replacing — shifting. A growing proportion of the moments when people used to type a query into Google now happen inside a conversation with an AI. Product comparisons. Category introductions. Brand evaluations. "What's a good [X] for [Y]?" used to produce ten blue links. Now it produces a paragraph or two, with the AI making recommendations rather than just listing options.

The difference matters. A search result is a link — users decide whether to click. An AI answer is a statement — the AI has already decided. If your brand isn't in the answer, or is represented inaccurately, you won't know unless you go looking.

What LLMO is

LLMO stands for LLM Optimization — the practice of designing content so that AI language models can read it accurately, summarize it correctly, and cite it usefully.

It is the discipline of writing and structuring content clearly enough that a system reading at scale can understand what you actually mean.

This works across three layers.

How content is written. AI systems process structure. Clear claims, specific details, consistent terminology. Content that hedges everything and promises nothing trains the AI to describe you as "a leading provider of innovative solutions." Which tells no one anything.

How websites are built. The architecture of a site — what information lives where, how pages relate to each other, what's in metadata and what's only in images — affects AI readability the same way it affects human readability, but with different failure modes. A piece of information buried in a decorative graphic doesn't exist for an AI reader.

How multilingual content works. If your brand operates in multiple languages, each version of your content trains AI systems separately. An accurate English description and a vague Japanese description produce different results for different audiences. LLMO across languages requires that each version is specific in its own right — not translated, not approximate.

What we build at UTAKATA LAB

The two types of work we do here come from the same question: what does it mean to design content that actually works?

The first is LLMO work. We are helping brands think about their content and site architecture from both sides: the human reading it now, and the AI synthesizing it next. This is strategy and structure work, applied to marketing copy, website design, and multilingual content across Japanese, English, and Chinese.

The second is embedding AI readability into interactive content — FAQs, personality tests, divination experiences, brand tools. These started as a separate interest, but the connection became obvious quickly. A well-designed quiz generates specific, structured outputs that are easy for both humans and AI to understand. That makes it one of the most LLMO-ready types of content that exists.

The question is how content works — for the person reading it now, and for the AI that reads it next. Build for both.
